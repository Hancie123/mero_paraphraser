Climate change represents one of the most pressing challenges facing humanity in the twenty-first century. Rising global temperatures have been linked to more frequent and severe weather events, including droughts, floods, and heatwaves (IPCC, 2021). These changes not only threaten ecosystems but also have profound implications for food security and public health. Moreover, the economic costs of climate-related disasters have increased substantially over the past two decades. This study examines the relationship between regional temperature anomalies and agricultural yields in South Asia. This analysis suggests that adaptation strategies may reduce, but not eliminate, projected losses.
